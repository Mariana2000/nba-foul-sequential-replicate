\label{sec:conclusion}

We develop an event-level framework for testing sequential dependence in NBA foul-call direction using play-by-play data (\sampleseasons{}). The strongest evidence concerns previous-call reversal: after a foul on the home team, the next foul is about five percentage points less likely to be on the home team; the association survives team and game fixed effects and is eliminated when foul order is shuffled within games (100 placebo draws).

Foul-count imbalance---period and game differentials---shows negative associations in pooled models but weaker and less clean interpretation under game fixed effects and placebo checks designed for order-defined predictors. Offensive fouls, analyzed separately, follow different dynamics and are excluded from the main sample.

We characterize the findings as sequential dependence in foul-call outcomes, not evidence of referee intent or referee-specific behavior. The pipeline offers a template for falsification-aware sequential analysis of officiating and related high-frequency sports decisions.
